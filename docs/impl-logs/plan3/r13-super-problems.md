## 1. Findings

### 1.1 Verified facts (F = fact, with source)
F1 [D] Eligibility: super earned on a temporary resident visa (not 405/410); you have left Australia; your visa has
   ceased (expired or cancelled); you "do not hold any other active Australian visa"; not an Australian or NZ citizen or
   permanent resident.
F2 [D] Online application is free. You can start and save it in Australia; you can only submit after leaving with no
   active visa. Paper: NAT 7204 sent to the fund; NAT 74880 for ATO-held super.
F3 [H] The online application does NOT mention myGov at all; [D] mentions myGov only for locating super ("after creating
   a myGov account and linking to the ATO"). [H]: "You don't have to provide your Australian tax file number (TFN)";
   without a TFN you must give your super account details (fund ABN, member number). The system can look up the ABN by
   fund name; Super Fund Lookup or the fund also give it.
F4 [H] Fund not found: select "Add application" on the "Add an application" page and start with the fund's ABN.
F5 [H] After submission an application "can be viewed, but not changed"; changes go to the fund directly. Saved
   applications reopen with a "shared secret" (question and answer, 1 to 20 characters, three attempts; forgotten: phone
   the ATO). Statuses: submitted, rejected, new application, not to be forwarded, transferred. The system does not show
   whether the fund has processed it. Eligibility check usually takes about 45 seconds.
F6 [H] If your temporary visa is still active after you leave you cannot submit; check your visa in VEVO. If the visa
   information shown is wrong, do not submit online; email DASPmail@ato.gov.au.
F7 [H] ATO from overseas: +61 2 6216 1111, 8:00 am to 5:00 pm Monday to Friday (Australian Eastern time), ask for 13 10 20.
   TIS National interpreters +61 3 9268 8332.
F8 [D] Online and $5,000 or more in a fund: the fund may require certified copies of ID. Paper and $5,000 or more: fund
   may require a Certification of Immigration Status (CoIS) from Home Affairs, which has a fee. Paper under $5,000: other
   evidence of departure and visa ceased; the fund may ask for a CoIS if you cannot provide it. "It's much easier to
   certify documents while you are in Australia." Online applications have status confirmed with Home Affairs
   automatically, no CoIS unless the fund directs.
F9 [R] Funds: online claims under $5,000 do not need standard client identification; paper claims need identification
   "regardless of the value". A fund must process and pay within 28 days of the request, including any additional
   information it needs.
F10 [N] Certifying outside Australia: consular staff at an Australian embassy, high commission or consulate; if none can
   be reached, a notary public, Justice of the Peace, judge, or registrar or deputy registrar of a court; if none, check
   whether the fund accepts other certification. Certifier sights the original and writes or stamps "certified true copy"
   with signature, printed name, qualification and date, on each page.
F11 [N] Paper under $5,000 immigration evidence: your visa or evidence the temporary visa is no longer in effect, or your
   passport with the departure stamp. Name changed since entering Australia: certified evidence of the change (e.g.
   marriage certificate), regardless of the amount.
F12 [F] Form 1194 requests a CoIS for a paper DASP; a fee applies; one form per person; returned by email to the address on
   the form; "Do not send ATO DASP claim forms to the Department". Online DASP already verifies status.
F13 [D] Payment: EFT to an Australian bank account (ATO: usually the most effective; keep an Australian account open);
   Australian dollar cheque; international money transfer for fund applications only, not all funds offer it, fees and
   currency conversion may apply. ATO-held super: only EFT to an Australian account in your name or cheque.
F14 [D] Unclaimed transfer: fund moves super to the ATO when 6 months or more have passed since you left AND the visa has
   ceased. ATO-held super can still be claimed as a DASP.
F15 [D] WHM rate 65% on the taxable component (taxed and untaxed element); tax-free component nil. Applies if the DASP
   includes contributions made while you held a 417 or 462 or an associated bridging visa, and then covers the whole
   payment. Each fund sets the rate for its own payment. DASP is not assessable income: not on the tax return.
F16 [D] You can authorise a registered tax agent (DASP online intermediary system) or another person (paper, written
   authority) to claim for you. Generally paid within 28 days of a completed application.
F17 Fund examples: [HP] form: outside Australia accepts Australian consular officers, foreign notary public, and some
   Australian-licensed professionals; other foreign-licensed certifiers are NOT accepted; certifications older than 12
   months, faxed or emailed copies are refused; non-English documents need an accredited translation; Hostplus pays EFT to
   an Australian account in AUD. [HP page] over $5,000: Hostplus DASP form plus certified passport copy plus ATO online
   application. [AW]: pays to an Australian account, by international transfer to an overseas account that accepts AUD,
   or AUD cheque; overseas payments may involve "foreign exchange margins and bank fees".
   INFERENCE: the ATO list [N] is wider than Hostplus's list, so "funds differ on certifiers" is correct and the guide
   must tell readers to check the fund before certifying.

### P1-A New guide: claim super without myGov  (Jo's core theme, zero competition)
slug: claim-super-without-mygov
title: Claim your super without myGov: what you actually need (51)
description: The ATO's DASP online application does not use myGov and does not even need your TFN. What it does need,
and what to do when you cannot find your fund. (150)
Body:
You do not need myGov to claim your super after a working holiday. The Departing Australia Superannuation Payment
(DASP) is claimed through the ATO's own free online application, which has no myGov login, and the ATO says you do not
even have to give your tax file number. Locked out of myGov, lost your Australian phone number for the SMS code, never
linked the ATO: none of that blocks the claim. ([D], [H])

## Where does myGov come in at all?
Only in finding your super. The ATO describes myGov linked to the ATO as one way to see every account in your name,
including super already moved to the ATO. The claim itself runs through the DASP online application, which is a
separate ATO system. ([D])

## What does the online application ask for instead?
Your name, date of birth, email, passport country and number, your visa details, and for each account the fund's ABN and
your member number. Your TFN is optional; without it you give the account details yourself. The system can look up a
fund's ABN from its name. ([H])

## What if I do not know my fund or member number?
Start with the places you worked: each employer paid into one fund, usually printed on your payslips. If the system does
not list a fund you know you had, choose "Add application" and start one with that fund's ABN. Finding every account is
explained in [how to find lost super](/blog/how-to-find-lost-superannuation). ([H])

## Can I phone the ATO from overseas instead?
Yes. From outside Australia call +61 2 6216 1111, 8:00 am to 5:00 pm Monday to Friday, Australian Eastern time, and ask
to be transferred to 13 10 20. An interpreter service is on +61 3 9268 8332. ([H])

## What still has to be true before I can submit?
You have left Australia, your 417 or 462 visa has expired or been cancelled, and you hold no other active Australian
visa. You can start and save the application before you fly; you submit it after. ([D]) If the system says your visa is
still active, see [visa still active: why you cannot claim your super yet](/blog/visa-still-active-cant-claim-super).

## Does the tax return work the same way?
It is a separate claim. Super comes from your fund, and over-withheld tax only comes back through a
[tax return](/tax-return), which you can also lodge from overseas without myGov access when you use someone who deals
with the ATO for you. See [the DASP claim, step by step](/superannuation#how-to-claim).
+ CLOSING.

### P1-B New guide: visa still active
slug: visa-still-active-cant-claim-super
title: Visa still active? Why you cannot claim your super yet (54)
description: A DASP can only be submitted once you have left and your visa has ceased, with no other visa active,
bridging visas included. How to check in VEVO and what to do. (155)
Body:
You cannot submit a super claim while any Australian visa in your name is still in effect, even after you have flown
home. The ATO's rule is that you have left Australia, your temporary visa has expired or been cancelled, and you hold no
other active Australian visa. If the online application says your visa is active, it is reading Home Affairs records,
and the fix is in your visa status, not in the form. ([D], [H])

## How do I check what the records show?
Use VEVO, the Department of Home Affairs visa check. The ATO points to VEVO for exactly this. If the expiry date there
is still in the future, the claim cannot be submitted yet. ([H])

## Which visas count as "another active visa"?
Any Australian visa. A second or third year working holiday visa granted before you left, a visa you applied for that
put you on a bridging visa, or a visa granted for a later trip all keep you ineligible while they are in effect. ([D])
For the tax rate, a bridging visa attached to a 417 or 462 is treated as a working holiday visa. ([D])

## Can I just wait for it to expire?
Yes, and many people do. Start and save the application now so it is ready, then submit once the visa has ceased. Keep
in mind that once six months have passed since you left and your visa has ceased, an unclaimed balance moves to the
ATO; it is still claimable there, but paid only to an Australian account or by cheque. ([D]) See
[what happens to unclaimed super](/blog/what-happens-to-unclaimed-super).

## Can I have the visa cancelled instead?
The ATO's DASP page links to the Department of Home Affairs page on cancelling a visa
(https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/cancelling-a-visa). Cancellation is an immigration decision, so read that page
before you ask for it, especially if you may want to come back to Australia. ([D])

## The visa details shown are wrong. What now?
Do not submit online. The ATO asks you to email DASPmail@ato.gov.au instead. ([H])

## Is a rejected claim lost money?
No. The super stays in the fund until a valid claim is made. The other common reasons are in
[DASP claim rejected](/blog/dasp-rejected-what-to-do), and who can claim is set out under
[who can claim a DASP](/superannuation#who-can-claim). + CLOSING.
Links: /superannuation#who-can-claim, /superannuation#while-in-australia, /blog/dasp-rejected-what-to-do,
/blog/what-happens-to-unclaimed-super, /blog/claim-super-without-mygov.
LEAVE OUT (unverified): how long Home Affairs takes to update a record; whether cancellation can be requested from
overseas and how; any effect of cancellation on future visas.

### P1-C New guide: certify documents overseas
slug: certify-documents-overseas-super-claim
title: Certified copies from overseas for a super claim (48)
description: Who can certify your passport for a DASP once you have left Australia, what the copy must say, and why
your fund's own list matters more than the ATO's. (146)
Body:
Once you have left Australia, the ATO's list of people who can certify copies for a super claim starts with consular
staff at an Australian embassy, high commission or consulate. If none is within reach, it accepts a notary public, a
Justice of the Peace, a judge, or a registrar or deputy registrar of a court. Your fund has the final say, and some
accept fewer people than the ATO lists. ([N])

## When will a fund ask for certified copies at all?
Mainly when it holds $5,000 or more, or when you apply on paper. On the online application a fund may want certified ID
for $5,000 or more; on paper a fund identifies you whatever the balance. ([D], [R])

## What must the certified copy show?
The certifier sees the original and writes or stamps "certified true copy" on each page, with their signature, printed
name, qualification and the date. ([N]) Funds can add rules: Hostplus, for example, refuses certifications more than 12
months old, faxed or emailed copies, and wants an accredited English translation of non-English documents. ([HP])

## Why is the fund's list the one that counts?
Because funds differ. Hostplus accepts a foreign notary public and Australian consular officers, but not other
certifiers licensed only outside Australia. ([HP]) A copy certified by someone your fund does not accept comes back,
and the claim waits. Ask the fund before you book anyone.

## Is it easier to do before I leave?
Yes. The ATO says so plainly: certifying is much easier while you are in Australia, and you can start and save the
online application before you fly. ([D]) See [what documents a DASP needs](/blog/dasp-documents-required).

## What if nobody on the list is near me?
The ATO says to check whether your fund will consider other certification. ([N]) Every fund decides this for itself.
Related: [why claims stall and how long they take](/blog/how-long-does-dasp-take) and
[the documents section of the DASP guide](/superannuation#documents). + CLOSING.

### P2-A New guide: DASP online application problems
slug: dasp-online-application-problems
title: DASP online application not working? Common snags (52)
description: Fund not listed, forgotten shared secret, a mistake after submitting, a status that will not move. What
the ATO's DASP system does and does not let you do. (149)
Body:
Most problems with the ATO's DASP online application come from three limits: it cannot be changed once submitted, it
only reopens with your shared secret, and it does not show whether your fund has acted. Knowing that, most snags have a
plain next step. ([H])

## My fund is not listed. Is the money gone?
No. Choose "Add application" and start one with the fund's ABN; the system can find the ABN from the fund's name. ([H])
Super that has already moved to the ATO is claimed through the same application. ([D]) See
[how to find lost super](/blog/how-to-find-lost-superannuation).

## I forgot my shared secret.
Saved applications reopen with a question and answer you chose (1 to 20 characters), with three attempts. If you
cannot remember it, phone the ATO: +61 2 6216 1111 from overseas. ([H]) If your details now differ from the ones you
first used, you can start a new application but cannot see the old ones. ([H])

## I made a mistake after submitting.
A submitted application can be viewed but not changed. Contact the fund directly to correct it. ([H])

## What do the statuses mean?
The system shows submitted, rejected, new application, not to be forwarded or transferred. It does not show whether the
fund has processed the claim; funds usually process within 28 days unless they need more information, and the ATO says
it cannot always meet its 28 day standard for ATO-held super. ([H]) After 28 days, contact the fund, or the ATO if it
holds the money. ([H]) More in [how long a DASP takes](/blog/how-long-does-dasp-take).

## It says I cannot submit.
Either you are still in Australia or a visa is still active; see
[visa still active](/blog/visa-still-active-cant-claim-super). If the visa details shown are wrong, email
DASPmail@ato.gov.au rather than submitting. ([H])

## Do I need myGov for any of this?
No: see [claim your super without myGov](/blog/claim-super-without-mygov) and [the DASP claim](/superannuation). + CLOSING.

### P2-B New guide: paid to a foreign bank account
slug: super-payment-overseas-bank-account
title: DASP to an overseas bank account: options and fees (51)
description: A fund can pay your super by Australian transfer, AUD cheque or, if it offers one, an international
transfer. ATO-held super goes to an Australian account only. (147)
Body:
Your super can reach a foreign bank account only if the fund that holds it offers an international money transfer, and
not all funds do. The other options are a transfer to an Australian bank account or an Australian dollar cheque. Super
already moved to the ATO is paid only to an Australian account in your name or by cheque. ([D])

## Should I close my Australian bank account before I leave?
The ATO suggests keeping it open, because a transfer to an Australian account is usually the most effective way to be
paid, and it is the only electronic route for ATO-held super. ([D]) Your tax refund also needs an Australian account,
see [the tax return](/tax-return).

## What does an international transfer cost?
The ATO says fees and currency conversion may apply. ([D]) Aware Super, for example, pays to an overseas account that
accepts Australian dollars and warns of foreign exchange margins and bank fees. ([AW]) Ask your fund before choosing.

## What details does the fund need for an overseas account?
On the paper form: the bank code, the bank's full name and address, the account name, your address as the bank holds
it, the account number or IBAN, and the currency. ([N]) Hostplus's own form pays only into an Australian account in
Australian dollars, which shows why you check fund by fund. ([HP])

## Does the 65% come off before or after the transfer?
Before. The fund withholds the tax and pays the rest; for working holiday makers it is 65% of the taxable component.
([D]) Worked figures are in [the 65% DASP rate explained](/blog/dasp-tax-rate-65-percent-explained).

## Several funds, several payments?
Yes, each fund pays separately and sets its own rate, so each one can arrive by a different route.
([D]) See [several super funds](/blog/super-multiple-funds-consolidation) and
[claiming from overseas](/superannuation#from-overseas). + CLOSING.

### P2-C New guide: fund asks for documents you do not have
slug: super-fund-documents-you-dont-have
title: Your super fund wants a document you don't have (51)
description: Renewed passport, changed name, no departure stamp, no CoIS. What a fund can accept instead for a DASP,
and when Home Affairs has to confirm your visa status. (153)
Body:
A fund that asks for a document you cannot produce usually has an alternative: the online application already confirms
your visa status with Home Affairs, and on paper the Department can issue a Certification of Immigration Status for a
fee. What cannot be skipped is proof that you are the person on the account. ([D], [F])

## I applied online. Why does the fund want visa evidence?
Usually it does not need to: online claims are checked with Home Affairs, and you only need a Certification of
Immigration Status if the fund tells you to. ([D])

## I am applying on paper and have no departure stamp.
Under $5,000, the fund can accept your visa or other evidence that it has ceased, or a passport with your departure
stamp, and may ask for a CoIS if you have neither. At $5,000 or more on paper, the fund may require the CoIS. ([D], [N])
It is requested with Home Affairs Form 1194, one per person, with a fee; the ATO claim form never goes to Home Affairs.
([F])

## I renewed my passport.
Tell the fund both passport numbers. Your account may have been opened against the old one; this is also a common reason
for [a rejected claim](/blog/dasp-rejected-what-to-do). (inference from existing guide; no official source: keep it as
advice, not a rule)

## My name changed since I was in Australia.
Funds need certified evidence of the change, such as a marriage certificate, whatever the amount. ([N]) Certifying from
abroad: [certified copies from overseas](/blog/certify-documents-overseas-super-claim).

## The fund has gone quiet.
A fund must process and pay within 28 days of the request, and that includes asking you for anything extra. ([R])
Keep a record of when you sent each document. More: [what documents a DASP needs](/blog/dasp-documents-required) and
[the DASP claim](/superannuation#documents). + CLOSING.
