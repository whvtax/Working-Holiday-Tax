How to read the evidence column:
* **GSC n**: the German query appears in our Queries export with n impressions over 3 months, and its average position.
* **SERP observed**: seen in a German language Google search on 5 Nov 2026 (who ranks is named).
* **native phrasing**: how German speakers word it, without a German GSC row. Where the English twin of the query has GSC demand, that is added as "EN twin GSC n".

Caveats. German query volume in our export is small: only about 25 German queries make the top 1,000 rows, so most German demand is hidden below the export cutoff. Page level numbers (impressions, position) come from the Pages sheet for the /de URL and are given as the current state. The export has no query by page pairing, so "ranks pos X" for a keyword means the query position from the Queries sheet; page positions are labelled "page".

Ranking German pages are travel blogs and portals (reiseuhu.de "australien backpacker steuer" from 2019, urlaubsguru.de/.at "work and travel tipps", citiesinsider.com /de guides, australia.com/de-de, lookuptax.com/docs/de), while English pages from ATO, super funds, Lawpath, etax and ITP fill the rest. ## 2. Gaps (clear demand, no matching page or section)

1. **rentenversicherung australien zurück / rente aus australien zurückholen** (native phrasing). German speakers think of super as the Australian "Rentenversicherung" or "Rente". None of the five DE service pages uses either word (0 occurrences); the word appears only inside blog bodies. Extend https://workingholidaytax.com.au/de/superannuation with one short section and one FAQ: "Ist Super die australische Rentenversicherung? Kann ich sie zurückholen?", answered in two sentences, linking to the DASP steps.
2. **steuern zurück australien work and travel** (SERP observed: reiseuhu.de and urlaubsguru.de/.at hold this with 2019 era travel content). This is the most common lay phrasing for the refund service and our home uses "Steuern zurück" only twice. Extend https://workingholidaytax.com.au/de with a visible H2 that uses "Steuern zurückholen nach Work and Travel" and a three line answer (who gets money back, typical reasons, how long).
3. **work and travel einkommen in deutschland angeben / progressionsvorbehalt work and travel** (native phrasing). Every German returning home asks whether Australian income must go on the German return. No DE page mentions "Progressionsvorbehalt" (0 occurrences). Extend https://workingholidaytax.com.au/de/blog/tax-obligations-after-leaving-australia with a short section that states what the Australian side covers, that the German return is a separate matter, and that a German Steuerberater or the Finanzamt answers it. Keep it to pointers, not German tax advice.
4. **backpacker steuer urteil / backpacker tax deutsche zurückzahlen** (SERP observed: tageskarte.io "Australien muss Rucksacktouristen Steuer zurückzahlen, viele Deutsche dürfen hoffen", theworld.org, SBS). The Addy ruling is only covered inside https://workingholidaytax.com.au/de/blog/tax-residency-working-holiday-makers. Extend https://workingholidaytax.com.au/de/blog/backpacker-tax-history-australia with a German specific section on what the ruling means for German nationals today, with the ATO source and date, and link to it from /de/blog/backpacker-tax-rate-australia.
5. **was kostet die steuererklärung australien / tax agent australien kosten** (native phrasing). Price is the second question after "how much do I get back". /de and /de/tax-return say the fee is flat, never a percentage, and agreed on WhatsApp, but there is no section that answers the cost question directly. Extend https://workingholidaytax.com.au/de/tax-return with a "Was kostet das?" FAQ using the confirmed fees ($220 with TFN, $385 with ABN, incl. GST), flat and never a share of the refund, plus the existing guarantee (difference refunded after the Notice of Assessment if the refund is below the fee). Never promise a refund.
