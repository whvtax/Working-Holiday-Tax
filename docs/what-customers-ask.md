## The single biggest theme: myGov / ATO access

This comes up constantly, and it is the thing most likely to eat your time and go wrong. Customers ask it in three different shapes:

The first shape is "I can't get into myGov / I've left Australia / I don't have an Australian ID, can you still help?" This one is pure opportunity. The answer is a confident yes: they never need myGov access because you handle everything through the ATO directly and the refund lands in their bank. Will now leads with exactly that reassurance, because it removes the single biggest reason a good lead walks away.

The second shape is the dangerous one: the customer starts asking you to actually troubleshoot a government login, an IHI application, or a Medicare Entitlement Statement that won't submit. Your most recent conversations show precisely how this goes wrong: one thread ran for hours trying to help a customer submit an IHI/MES through Services Australia, it failed anyway, and it soaked up a huge amount of time for no gain. Per your instruction, Will will now **never** answer, troubleshoot, or give step-by-step help on myGov, ATO login, myGovID/Digital ID, IHI, linking, or exemption-form errors. It gives only the reassurance that they don't need that access, and it hands anything deeper to a human. This is now a hard boundary in Will's rules, not a soft preference.

The third shape appears *after* lodging: "it still shows as not lodged on my myGov." Left unanswered this reads like something went wrong. Will now explains calmly that you lodge through your own registered agent systems, so their personal account can take a day or two to update, and offers to check if it hasn't after 48 hours.

## The free-estimate vs pay-upfront tension

This is your most important commercial inconsistency, and your customers have noticed it. Your public marketing promises a **free eligibility check** and "we'll tell you your estimated refund before you decide." But when someone has *already started their own return* (via myGov), your actual practice is to require the **$220 upfront** before you review it. One customer put the two side by side and asked you to explain the contradiction directly.

Both positions are defensible, they just need to be one clear story. Will now handles it cleanly: the free check is for people who want you to prepare and lodge the whole return; if they've already done the work themselves and just want it reviewed or corrected, the fee is upfront, protected by the "not eligible, full refund of the $220" guarantee. Worth deciding at the business level whether that distinction is stated anywhere public, because right now it only surfaces once a customer pushes.

## Tax residency is where refunds are won or lost — and TikTok is confusing people

Residency (working holiday maker vs Australian resident for tax purposes) shows up in a large share of chats, and it swings the refund dramatically. Two recurring problems: your form actively **blocks** WHV holders with a "you paid the correct tax, you don't qualify" message, which makes people give up before you ever see them; and several customers arrive having seen **TikTok advice** telling them to "just select resident," so they're second-guessing the form.

Will now treats residency carefully: it never determines residency itself (that stays a human/professional judgement), it points them to the explanation on the form, it reassures them that the form's rejection is not the final word, and it routes genuine uncertainty to a person rather than guessing. The strategic note for you: that hard block on the form is almost certainly costing you leads who would qualify as residents. It may be worth softening the form copy so it invites a review instead of ending the conversation.

## The recurring questions, in rough order of how often they appear

Pricing and "is it worth it": customers want to know it's a flat fee (not a percentage), whether they pay if the refund is small, whether there are hidden extras, and occasionally they ask for a discount. Will holds the $220 / $385 line, never negotiates, and always frames the guarantee so the fee reads as low-risk.

"Is this legit?": found you on Instagram/TikTok, a bit hesitant. One customer even flagged that your WhatsApp business name was **misspelled** ("hoiday" instead of "holiday") and that people were asking in a backpacker group whether you're real. That's a live trust issue worth fixing at the source. Will reassures with the registered-agent supervision and the client agreement.

Documents: why you need a passport, why a bank statement, which bank number to use (card vs account), and reassurance that you don't need their payslips because you see everything through the ATO. These are quick wins Will now answers instantly.

Deductions and expenses: laundry, phone, uniform, working-from-home, travel between work sites, "I don't have receipts." This is genuinely personalised tax territory, so Will gives only the general shape ("things that helped you earn income, send me what you have") and leaves the actual determinations to your team, which is where they belong.

Medicare: whether the levy exemption is included in the fee (it is), and what the exemption is. Will explains the concept and that you guide eligible clients through it, but stops short of walking anyone through the IHI/Services Australia mechanics, for the reasons above.

Super (DASP): several ask if they can claim super while still in the country. The factual answer is no, only after leaving Australia with the visa expired, and Will now states that and offers to help when the time comes.

Timing and process: "how does the money reach me," "how long does it take" (about 14 business days), "can I pay after my refund," and post-payment signing hiccups (the Xero link asking them to log in). Will answers the simple ones and hands off the genuinely account-specific ones.

## Two operational patterns worth a business decision

**Scope creep on fixed-fee returns.** One of your recent threads shows a return that ballooned well beyond a standard one (many receipts, back-and-forth over deductions), where you tried mid-way to raise the price from $220 to $330. The customer pushed back hard on being re-quoted after already sending everything, and it became a difficult exchange. Will will never invent or change a price or attempt an upsell like this on its own; it holds the quoted fee and routes any pricing exception to you. If variable-effort returns are a real cost for you, the cleaner fix is a defined up-front rule (e.g. a complex-return tier stated before work starts) rather than a renegotiation once the customer feels finished.

**Returning customers and reviews.** Your follow-up flow (Google review requests, "message me after July 1") clearly works and brings people back year over year. Will is built to treat a returning customer as continuing where they left off, never as a cold new lead.
